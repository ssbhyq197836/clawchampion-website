Know what you are playing

Real remote coin pusher vs virtual coin pusher

Both can be played on a phone, but they describe different kinds of games. ClawChampion's coin pusher rooms connect to physical arcade machines through live video and remote controls.

The practical difference

What to checkReal remote machineFully virtual game
PlayfieldA physical machine and physical moving parts.A computer-generated playfield.
What the phone showsLive video from a machine room, with app controls and digital interface elements.Rendered game graphics, whether or not they imitate a real arcade cabinet.
After a control inputA command is sent to the remote hardware and the player watches its response on video.Game software calculates and displays the response.
ConnectionVideo and controls depend on a network connection and can experience delay or interruption.The game may still require internet, but no remote physical machine has to respond.

Where ClawChampion fits

The App Store listing and Google Play listing describe real remote arcade machines. The phone interface adds controls and progress information, but the room video shows physical hardware.

This distinction describes the machine, not the reward rules. For GP costs and other current terms, use the app's room instructions and official policies.

Questions to ask of any remote arcade app

Does the app identify a specific live room? Can you watch the machine react to a control input? Does it explain the room's cost and how to report an interrupted session? These checks are more useful than assuming every phone coin pusher is either fully physical or fully simulated.

Live video can lag. If a room freezes or a control appears not to move the machine, stop and report the room, time, UID and any affected GP to customer service.
